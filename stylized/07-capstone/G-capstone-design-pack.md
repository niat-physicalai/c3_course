<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">This Pack Is Your Proposal</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>This Design Pack is exactly what you submit when you apply for the funded build.</strong> A reviewer will open one folder, read one README, and ask three questions: Is this a real problem? Is the design complete enough to build? Does this person know where the risks are? This unit turns your work into a pack that answers all three quickly, checks it against a rubric, and ends with the most honest section of all: a comparison with the reference watch, including where your design is better.</div>

<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">What You Will Be Able to Do After This Reading</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Keep every file in a format a reviewer can open without your tools: diagrams as images or Mermaid text, spreadsheets as .xlsx or .csv, KiCad and CAD files alongside PDF or image exports of the key views.</div>

<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">The README: One Page That Explains the Rest</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:14px 0 8px 0;">The README is the first thing a reviewer reads, and often the only thing they read carefully. Keep it to one page:</div>

```markdown
# <Product name> — Design Pack v1.0

## The problem
Two sentences, from your Part 2 problem statement.

## The product
Three sentences: what it is, who wears or uses it, what it does.
One image: the coloured CAD model with the board inside.

## Key numbers
| Battery life (usage pattern UP-1) | Size (W × L × H) | Unit cost at 1 / 10 |
|---|---|---|

## Status of every check
| Check | Result | Where |
|---|---|---|
| ERC | 0 errors | 04-schematic-and-pcb/ |
| DRC | 0 errors, N accepted warnings | 04-schematic-and-pcb/ |
| Interference | 0 overlaps | 07-mechanical/ |
| Fab DFM | resolved | 08-manufacturing/ |
| Simulation | link | 06-firmware-and-simulation/ |

## Top three risks
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...

## How to read this pack
One line per folder.
```

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">The "top three risks" section matters more than it looks. A reviewer who sees you have named your own risks trusts the rest of the pack more, not less.</div>

<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">The Verification Log</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">In the verification stack page at the start of the course, you were asked to record every check: what, when and the result. It is the evidence that your design has been checked by something other than your own confidence.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">For a real repository that keeps a product's firmware, KiCad project, fabrication files and images together behind one README, look at <a href="https://github.com/niat-physicalai/esp_watch">esp\_watch's repository</a> and its <a href="https://github.com/niat-physicalai/esp_watch/tree/main/pcb/esp_Watch">KiCad folder</a>. <div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Throughout the course, the reference watch, esp\_watch, has been used as a worked example, including the places where it got things wrong. Now compare your design with it deliberately, section by section.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">For every section, answer three questions:</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:14px 0 8px 0;">1. <strong>Where did you converge?</strong> You made the same choice. Was it for the same reason?</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:14px 0 8px 0;">2. <strong>Where did you diverge?</strong> You made a different choice.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:14px 0 8px 0;">3. <strong>Can you justify each divergence?</strong> Either your product's requirements are different, or your choice is better, or you have learned something.</div>

<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">Where the Reference Is Weak, Say So</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">esp\_watch is the course author's own design, with known issues. Criticise it wherever your design does better.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">These are the reference watch's recorded weak points, all of which appeared in earlier units:</div>

<ul style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin:6px 0;">​<strong>Thickness.</strong> The board with its parts is 14.044 mm tall, because the display sits on a female header above the motion sensor (E2).</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">​<strong>An obsolete motion sensor</strong>, a clone on its module, kept for version 1 (F0).</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">​<strong>A lid held by an interference fit</strong>, sensitive to print accuracy and loosening with use (E3).</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">​<strong>Measured on a breadboard, modelled on paper.</strong> Its bus measurements come from the breadboard prototype, and every current figure is modelled; none was measured (B3).</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">​<strong>Several design decisions not yet recorded</strong>, such as the sensor window and sweat protection (E4).</li></ul>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">If your design avoids any of these, say how. If it repeats one, say why that was acceptable for your product.</div>

<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">Part 4 — Version 2, on One Page</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">The last page of the pack looks forward. It is short, and it is where a reviewer sees whether you understand your own design.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:14px 0 8px 0;">Use this structure:</div>

```markdown
# What I would change in version 2

## Changes forced by risk
Things that must change for the product to survive: an obsolete part, a failure mode
with no detection, a requirement the design only just meets.

## Changes that improve the product
Things that would make it better: thinner, longer battery life, cheaper at volume.

## Things I would test first in the funded build
The gaps your simulations and CAD could not close: what you would measure first
on real hardware, and what result would change the design.
```

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">For every item, give the reason in one line and trace it to a requirement, a check or a cost.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">For comparison, a version 2 page for esp\_watch, written from its open issues, might include: replacing the obsolete MPU-6050 (TDK names the ICM-42670-P as its recommended alternate), which is a new driver behind the existing interface; rethinking the module stack to reduce thickness, perhaps towards C0's concept C; a repeatable lid fastening in place of the interference fit; and, first on the list for the build, measuring real current in every mode, because every figure so far is modelled.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">The "test first" section matters most. Your pack has been checked in every way that needs no hardware; the build checks the rest, so say exactly what to measure first.</div>

<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">Applying What You Have Learned</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>1. Build the folder structure</strong> in your design pack's Git repository and move every deliverable into its section. Tag the commit whose fabrication files you would send to the fab house (<a href="../01-system-architecture/REF-version-control.md">Version Control</a>, Step 5).</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>2. Write the README</strong>, including key numbers, the status of every check and your top three risks.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>3. Compile the verification log</strong> from every check you have run.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>4. Review against the rubric.</strong> Fix every "no" you can; list the rest as known gaps in the README.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>5. Write the reference review</strong>: converge, diverge and justify, for every section.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>Deliverable:</strong> the complete Design Pack folder.</div>

<div style="background:linear-gradient(90deg,#dbeafe 0%,#ffffff 100%);border-left:6px solid #1e3a8a;border-radius:6px;padding:10px 16px;margin:34px 0 16px 0;font-size:22px;font-weight:700;color:#1e3a8a;">Check Your Understanding</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>1.</strong> A reviewer has ten minutes for your Design Pack. What should they read first, and what must it contain?</div>

<ul style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin:6px 0;">A. The KiCad project, because it is the most technical.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">B. The one-page README: the problem, the product, key numbers, the status of every check, and the top three risks.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">C. The firmware source code.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">D. The BOM.</li></ul>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Answer</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>B.</strong> The README is the map of the pack, and it tells a reviewer whether the rest is worth their time. <strong>A</strong>, <strong>C</strong> and <strong>D</strong> are important evidence, but each covers only one part of the design.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>2.</strong> Your design uses a different connectivity route from the reference watch. What makes that divergence convincing?</div>

<ul style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin:6px 0;">A. Saying your choice is more modern.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">B. Tracing it to a requirement the reference does not have, and showing evidence, such as a power calculation, that it works.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">C. Choosing the same route to avoid questions.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">D. Not mentioning it.</li></ul>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Answer</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>B.</strong> A requirement plus evidence is a justification. <strong>A</strong> is a preference. <strong>C</strong> avoids the question rather than answering it. <strong>D</strong> leaves a reviewer to wonder.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>3.</strong> Your rubric review finds that DRC has three unexplained warnings. What should you do?</div>

<ul style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin:6px 0;">A. Delete the rubric item.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">B. Examine each warning; fix it or write the reason it is acceptable, then update the README's check status.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">C. Mark the item "Y" because there are no errors.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">D. Re-run DRC until they disappear.</li></ul>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Answer</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>B.</strong> An accepted warning needs a written reason, as C3 taught. <strong>A</strong> and <strong>C</strong> hide the gap. <strong>D</strong> does not change the design, so the warnings will not disappear.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>4.</strong> Your current budget is modelled, and your battery-life requirement is met with only 10 % margin. Where does this belong in your version 2 page?</div>

<ul style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin:6px 0;">A. Nowhere; the requirement is met.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">B. Under "things I would test first": measure real current in every mode, and state what result would force a bigger cell or lower sleep current.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">C. Under "changes that improve the product", as longer battery life.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">D. Only in the README's key numbers.</li></ul>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Answer: <strong>B.</strong> A modelled figure with thin margin is a risk only hardware can close, so it is tested first. <strong>A</strong> treats a model as a measurement. <strong>C</strong> calls a risk an improvement. <strong>D</strong> reports the number but hides the risk.</div>

<ul style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin:6px 0;">A. To make the page longer.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">B. Because nothing was built in this course, so the first real measurements are where the design's remaining risks are resolved; naming them shows you know where the gaps are.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">C. Because reviewers require a test plan in every proposal.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">D. Because the design will certainly fail.</li></ul>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Answer</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>B.</strong> A design checked only on a laptop still has things only hardware can confirm, such as real current and real sensor accuracy. Naming them is a sign of understanding. <strong>A</strong> is not a reason. <strong>C</strong> may be true of some reviewers, but it is not why. <strong>D</strong> is too pessimistic; the point is to find out quickly.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>5.</strong> In your reference review you find a divergence you cannot justify. What is the best response?</div>

<ul style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;padding-left:22px;"><li style="margin:6px 0;">A. Remove the row.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">B. Mark it "cannot justify", work out what the reference got right, fix your design if needed, and record what you learned.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">C. Change the reference's description to match yours.</li><li style="margin:6px 0;">D. Leave it as it is and hope the reviewer does not notice.</li></ul>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">Answer</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:10px 0;">​<strong>B.</strong> An honest "cannot justify" followed by a fix is a strong result. <strong>A</strong> and <strong>D</strong> hide it. <strong>C</strong> misrepresents the reference.</div>

<div style="text-align:justify;line-height:1.7;margin:14px 0 8px 0;font-weight:700;">A design is finished not when it looks right, but when every requirement is traced, every check is recorded, and every remaining risk is named.</div>
